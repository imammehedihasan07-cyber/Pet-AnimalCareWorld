Complete Rabbit Care, Diet, and Housing Guide

Rabbits make wonderful, affectionate, and quiet companion pets. However, unlike dogs or cats, they are exotic animals with unique physiological and dietary requirements. Understanding their sensitive digestive system is the key to ensuring a long and healthy life.

1. The Golden Rule of Rabbit Diet (Hay & Greens)

A rabbit's digestive tract relies heavily on continuous fiber intake to function properly.

  • Timothy Hay: Fresh grass hay (like Timothy or Orchard grass) should make up 80-90% of their daily diet and must be available 24/7.
  • Fresh Vegetables: Provide a cup of fresh leafy greens daily (such as romaine lettuce, cilantro, and parsley). Avoid iceberg lettuce.
  • Pellets: Feed high-fiber pellets in strictly measured small portions, as overfeeding leads to obesity.
Crucial Dietary Warning: Never feed rabbits high-carbohydrate human foods like bread, crackers, cookies, or excess grains. These cause fatal disruptions in their gut flora.

2. Safe Housing & Environment Setup

Whether housed indoors or outdoors, rabbits need adequate space to stretch, hop, and stand upright.

  • Enclosure Size: The cage or pen must be at least 4 times the length of the rabbit. Wire-bottom cages should be avoided to prevent painful foot sores (sore hocks).
  • Litter Training: Rabbits can be easily litter-trained using paper-based, non-toxic litter pellets.
  • Chew Proofing: Rabbits love to chew. Protect electrical cords and furniture legs from their sharp teeth.

3. Social Behavior & Exercise

Rabbits are prey animals that require mental stimulation and trust-building.

  • Allow at least 3-4 hours of supervised out-of-cage exercise daily in a bunny-proofed room.
  • Provide tunnels, chew toys made of untreated wood, and cardboard boxes to satisfy their natural instincts.

4. Health Maintenance & Warning Signs

Because they mask illnesses, look out for subtle signs of sickness such as reduced appetite, sitting hunched in a corner, or reduced fecal output. Spaying or neutering is strongly recommended at an early age to prevent reproductive cancers and behavioral issues.
