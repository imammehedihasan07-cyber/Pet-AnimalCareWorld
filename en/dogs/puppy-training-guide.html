Puppy Training Basics: Essential First Steps

👨‍⚕️
Reviewed by: Imam Mehedi Hasan, VFA
Veterinary Field Assistant & Livestock Medical Practitioner
Last Updated: August 2026

Key Takeaways

  • Start training the day you bring your puppy home; consistency is the key to success.
  • Positive reinforcement (treats and praise) works significantly better than punishment.
  • Puppies have small bladders; take them out for potty breaks every 1-2 hours.
  • Proper socialization before 16 weeks of age prevents future behavioral and fear issues.

Bringing a new puppy home is exciting, but it also comes with a lot of responsibility. A well-trained dog is a happy, safe, and welcome member of the family. The foundation of good behavior is established in the first few months of a puppy's life.

1. Potty Training (Housebreaking)

Patience and routine are your best tools for potty training.

  • Establish a Routine: Take your puppy outside immediately after they wake up, after eating, after playing, and before bedtime. As a rule of thumb, a puppy can hold their bladder for one hour per every month of age.
  • Designate a Spot: Always take them to the same area outside. The scent will encourage them to go.
  • Reward Immediately: Praise them and give a small treat the exact moment they finish doing their business outside.
  • Accidents Happen: Never punish your puppy for an indoor accident. Simply clean it up thoroughly with an enzymatic cleaner so they don't smell the spot and use it again.

2. The Importance of Socialization

Socialization means exposing your puppy to different people, environments, sounds, and other animals in a positive way. The critical window for this is between 3 and 16 weeks of age.

  • Introduce them to different surfaces (grass, carpet, tile).
  • Let them hear normal household noises (vacuum, washing machine) at a low volume initially.
  • Ensure all early interactions with other dogs are safe and positive. (Note: Avoid public dog parks until your puppy is fully vaccinated as per your veterinarian's schedule.)

3. Teaching Basic Commands

Keep training sessions short (5-10 minutes) so your puppy doesn't lose focus or get frustrated.

  • Sit: Hold a treat close to their nose and slowly move your hand up and back over their head. Their bottom will naturally hit the floor. Say "Sit" and reward.
  • Come: Start indoors in a quiet room. Squat down, open your arms, and say "Come" in an enthusiastic voice. Reward heavily when they reach you.

4. Managing Puppy Biting

Puppies explore the world with their mouths, and teething causes discomfort. It is crucial to teach bite inhibition early.

  • If your puppy bites you too hard during play, let out a high-pitched "Ouch!" and immediately stop playing for 10-15 seconds.
  • Always redirect their biting to an appropriate chew toy. Praise them when they chew the toy instead of your hands or furniture.
