Dog Skin & Coat Care: How to Prevent Hair Fall

👨‍⚕️
Reviewed by: Imam Mehedi Hasan, VFA
Veterinary Field Assistant & Livestock Medical Practitioner
Last Updated: August 2026

Key Takeaways

  • Normal shedding is natural, but excessive hair fall and dandruff often indicate nutritional deficiencies or skin infections.
  • Omega-3 and Omega-6 fatty acids are essential for maintaining a shiny coat and healthy skin barrier.
  • Regular brushing removes dead hair and distributes natural skin oils evenly.
  • Fleas, ticks, and fungal infections (like ringworm) are major culprits behind severe coat damage.

Finding dog hair all over your furniture and clothes is a common struggle for pet owners. While some shedding is a normal biological process, excessive hair fall, bald patches, and stubborn dandruff point to underlying health or skincare issues. Understanding the root cause is the first step toward a healthy coat.

1. Common Causes of Hair Fall & Dandruff

Hair loss (alopecia) and flaking skin can be triggered by several factors:

  • Nutritional Deficiencies: Lack of quality proteins, vitamins, and essential fatty acids makes hair shafts weak and brittle.
  • Seasonal Shedding: Many breeds shed heavily during spring and autumn as temperatures change.
  • Allergies: Food sensitivities or environmental allergens (pollen, dust mites) cause intense itching, leading to scratching and hair loss.
  • Frequent Bathing: Using harsh human shampoos strips the skin of natural oils, causing severe dryness and dandruff.

2. The Role of Nutrition in Coat Health

A shiny coat starts from the inside out.

  • Omega Fatty Acids: Supplementing with fish oil or feeding diets rich in Omega-3 and Omega-6 fatty acids nourishes the skin and reduces inflammation.
  • High-Quality Protein: Hair is made almost entirely of protein. Ensure your dog's diet features a high-grade animal protein source as the primary ingredient.

3. Proper Grooming Practices

Consistent brushing is your best defense against matting and uncontrolled shedding.

  • Brush your dog 2-3 times a week (daily for long-haired breeds) to stimulate blood circulation in the skin.
  • Use a dog-specific, mild, or oatmeal-based shampoo during baths, and ensure they are dried thoroughly to prevent fungal growth.
Veterinary Note: Avoid using human anti-dandruff shampoos on dogs unless specifically prescribed by a vet, as they can severely disrupt the pH balance of canine skin.

4. Parasites & Medical Conditions

Fleas, ticks, and mange mites cause severe irritation and hair thinning. Fungal infections like ringworm manifest as circular patches of hair loss with scaly skin. If you notice raw spots, persistent scratching, or foul odor, professional medical evaluation is required.
