Complete Kitten Feeding Guide & Nutrition Schedule

👨‍⚕️
Reviewed by: Imam Mehedi Hasan, VFA
Veterinary Field Assistant & Livestock Medical Practitioner
Last Updated: August 2026

Key Takeaways

  • Never give cow's milk to kittens; it causes severe diarrhea. Use formulated Kitten Milk Replacer (KMR).
  • Newborns (0-4 weeks) need feeding every 2-4 hours round the clock.
  • Weaning onto solid food begins around 4 weeks of age.
  • Kittens require 2-3 times more calories and protein than adult cats for healthy growth.

Proper nutrition during a kitten’s first year is critical. This rapid growth phase dictates their bone strength, immune system health, and overall development. Whether you are caring for a nursing mother, bottle-feeding an orphan, or bringing home an 8-week-old kitten, this guide covers what you need to know.

1. Feeding Newborn Kittens (0-4 Weeks)

If the mother cat is present and healthy, she will provide all the necessary nutrition. Newborns must nurse within the first 24 hours to receive colostrum, which contains vital maternal antibodies.

For orphaned kittens:

  • What to feed: Use a commercial Kitten Milk Replacer (KMR).
  • Schedule: Kittens under 2 weeks old need to be bottle-fed every 2 hours, day and night. At 3-4 weeks, feeding can be extended to every 4-5 hours.
  • Technique: Always feed kittens on their bellies (like they would nurse from their mother), never on their backs, to prevent milk from entering their lungs (aspiration pneumonia).
Important Warning: Do not feed kittens cow's milk, goat's milk, or human baby formula. Cats lack the enzyme to properly digest lactose, which leads to life-threatening dehydration and diarrhea.

2. The Weaning Process (4-8 Weeks)

At around 4 weeks of age, kittens will start showing interest in solid food.

  • The Slurry: Mix wet kitten food with KMR to create a loose, soupy consistency (gruel). Serve it in a shallow dish.
  • Transition: Over the next few weeks, gradually reduce the amount of milk and increase the solid food until they are eating only wet/dry kitten food by week 8.

3. Feeding Growing Kittens (8 Weeks to 1 Year)

By 8 weeks, kittens should be fully weaned and ready for a complete and balanced kitten formula.

  • Wet vs. Dry: A combination is usually best. Wet food provides necessary hydration (since cats naturally have a low thirst drive), while dry kibble is convenient for free-feeding.
  • Frequency: Feed 3-4 small meals a day. Their stomachs are small, but their energy requirements are massive.
  • Hydration: Always ensure fresh, clean water is available in shallow bowls.

4. Foods You Must Avoid

Keep these common household foods away from your kitten, as they are toxic to felines:

  • Onions, garlic, and chives (causes anemia)
  • Chocolate and caffeine
  • Grapes and raisins (causes kidney failure)
  • Raw meat or fish (risk of Salmonella and E. coli)
