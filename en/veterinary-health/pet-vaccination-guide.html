Complete Pet Vaccination Guide & Schedule for Dogs and Cats

👨‍⚕️
Reviewed by: Imam Mehedi Hasan, VFA
Veterinary Field Assistant & Livestock Medical Practitioner
Last Updated: August 2026

Key Takeaways

  • Vaccines protect pets from highly contagious and fatal diseases like Parvovirus and Rabies.
  • Puppies and kittens should start their vaccination series between 6 to 8 weeks of age.
  • Core vaccines are essential for all pets, regardless of their lifestyle or location.
  • Booster shots are required to maintain long-term immunity throughout the pet's adult life.

Preventative care is the cornerstone of veterinary medicine. Just like humans, dogs and cats are susceptible to a variety of dangerous viral and bacterial infections. Vaccinating your pet is the most effective and least expensive way to keep them healthy and prevent the spread of fatal diseases.

1. Why Are Vaccinations Important?

Vaccines contain antigens that mimic a disease organism. When injected, they stimulate the pet's immune system to produce antibodies. If the pet is ever exposed to the real disease, their immune system is fully prepared to fight it off completely or significantly reduce its severity.

2. Essential (Core) Vaccines for Dogs

Core vaccines are considered vital to all dogs based on the risk of exposure, the severity of the disease, or transmissibility to humans.

  • Rabies: A fatal viral disease that attacks the central nervous system. It is transmissible to humans (zoonotic) and legally required in most regions.
  • DAPP (or DHPP): This is a combination vaccine that protects against four deadly diseases:
    • Distemper: A severe viral illness causing respiratory, gastrointestinal, and neurological issues.
    • Adenovirus (Hepatitis): Causes severe liver damage.
    • Parvovirus: A highly contagious and often fatal virus that causes severe vomiting and bloody diarrhea, especially in puppies.
    • Parainfluenza: A respiratory virus contributing to kennel cough.

3. Essential (Core) Vaccines for Cats

Even indoor-only cats require core vaccines, as some viruses can be carried inside on your shoes or clothes.

  • Rabies: Essential and often legally required for felines as well.
  • FVRCP: A combination vaccine protecting against:
    • Feline Viral Rhinotracheitis (FVR): A severe upper respiratory infection.
    • Calicivirus (C): Causes respiratory issues and painful oral ulcers.
    • Panleukopenia (P): Also known as feline distemper, a highly fatal virus that attacks blood cells and the intestinal tract.

4. Standard Vaccination Schedule

Puppies and kittens receive antibodies from their mother's milk, which wear off over time. A series of vaccines is needed to ensure their own immune system takes over.

  • 6-8 Weeks: First round of core vaccines (DAPP for dogs, FVRCP for cats).
  • 10-12 Weeks: Second round of core vaccines.
  • 14-16 Weeks: Final round of core puppy/kitten vaccines + First Rabies vaccine.
  • Adult Boosters: A booster is given 1 year after the puppy/kitten series, and then usually every 1 to 3 years depending on the vaccine type and local laws.

5. Possible Side Effects & When to Worry

Mild side effects like lethargy, a slight fever, or tenderness at the injection site are normal for 24-48 hours. However, severe allergic reactions (anaphylaxis) require immediate emergency care.

Emergency Warning: If your pet experiences facial swelling, hives, vomiting, diarrhea, or difficulty breathing within a few hours of receiving a vaccine, contact an emergency veterinarian immediately. This is a severe allergic reaction.
