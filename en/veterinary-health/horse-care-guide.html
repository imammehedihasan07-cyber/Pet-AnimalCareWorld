Basic Horse Care, Nutrition, and Stable Management

👨‍⚕️
Reviewed by: Imam Mehedi Hasan, VFA
Veterinary Field Assistant & Livestock Medical Practitioner
Last Updated: August 2026

Key Takeaways

  • Horses require constant access to clean water and high-quality forage (hay/grass).
  • Stable hygiene is essential to prevent hoof rot and respiratory issues.
  • Daily grooming builds a bond and helps detect health issues like skin wounds early.
  • Colic (abdominal pain) is a common, life-threatening emergency in horses; watch for restless behavior.

1. Nutrition & Feeding

Horses have a specialized digestive system suited for small, frequent meals rather than large, infrequent ones.

  • Forage First: Hay or grass should be the primary component of a horse's diet.
  • Clean Water: An average adult horse drinks 5-10 gallons of water daily, depending on weather and activity level.
  • Salt Blocks: Provide access to salt/mineral blocks to maintain electrolyte balance.

2. Stable & Hoof Management

A clean stable is non-negotiable for equine health.

  • Bedding: Clean bedding daily to remove waste, reducing ammonia fumes that damage respiratory tracks.
  • Hoof Care: Pick out hooves daily to remove debris, and get a professional farrier to trim hooves every 6-8 weeks to prevent lameness.

3. Understanding Colic

Colic is not a specific disease but a general term for abdominal pain. It is one of the most common causes of death in horses.

  • Signs: Pawing at the ground, frequently looking at their flanks, rolling, sweating, and loss of appetite.
  • First Aid: If you suspect colic, remove all food, walk the horse slowly, and call a veterinarian immediately.
Emergency Warning: If a horse is violently rolling and unable to stand, this is a severe colic emergency. Do not wait for symptoms to pass; seek immediate veterinary assistance.

4. Routine Care

Daily grooming is more than just making the horse look nice. It is a vital health check where you inspect for swellings, skin rashes, cuts, or ticks that might otherwise go unnoticed.
