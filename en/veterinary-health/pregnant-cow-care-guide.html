Pregnant Cow Care & Calving Management Guide

Proper management of a pregnant cow (dam) is critical for successful dairy farming. The final trimester (gestation period is roughly 283 days) requires specialized care to ensure a healthy calf and a smooth lactation cycle thereafter.

1. Nutritional Needs During Gestation

During the last two months of pregnancy, the fetus grows rapidly, putting high metabolic demands on the cow.

  • Provide high-quality green fodder supplemented with balanced mineral mixtures to prevent milk fever and metabolic disorders.
  • Ensure a continuous supply of fresh, clean drinking water to support fetal fluid maintenance.

2. Managing the Dry Period

Stopping milking roughly 60 days before the expected calving date (the dry period) allows the udder tissue to rest and regenerate for the next lactation cycle.

  • Maintain a clean, dry, and well-ventilated maternity pen to prevent udder infections (mastitis).

3. Signs of Impending Calving

Recognizing the onset of labor helps farmers provide timely assistance:

  • Enlargement of the udder and relaxation of pelvic ligaments.
  • Swelling and mucous discharge from the vulva.
  • Restlessness, isolation from the herd, and tail lifting.

4. Calving Complications & Emergency Care

Most cows calve unassisted, but dystocia (difficult birth) requires immediate field intervention.

Emergency Warning: If the cow strains vigorously for over 2 hours without delivering the calf, or if abnormal presentations (such as legs back or head turned) occur, contact a veterinary practitioner immediately.
