Goat Care, Feeding, and Disease Prevention Guide

👨‍⚕️
Reviewed by: Imam Mehedi Hasan, VFA
Veterinary Field Assistant & Livestock Medical Practitioner
Last Updated: August 2026

Key Takeaways

  • Goats require a clean, dry shed with good ventilation to prevent respiratory illnesses.
  • PPR (Peste des Petits Ruminants) is the most critical viral disease for goats and must be vaccinated against.
  • Provide a mixture of forage, green grass, and limited concentrates for optimal growth.
  • Goats are highly sensitive to internal parasites (worms); routine deworming is essential.

1. Proper Housing & Sanitation

Goats are prone to pneumonia and foot rot if their living environment is damp or poorly ventilated.

  • Elevation: If possible, use raised slatted floors to keep the area dry and allow waste to fall through.
  • Ventilation: Ensure air circulation but protect them from direct cold drafts in winter.
  • Cleanliness: Regularly remove manure to minimize ammonia buildup and fly infestation.

2. Nutrition & Feeding Strategy

Goats are browsers, not grazers. Their digestive systems function best with a variety of roughage.

  • Forage: Access to clean grazing land, brush, and tree leaves.
  • Water: Goats are extremely picky about water. Always provide clean, fresh water.
  • Minerals: Provide free-choice mineral blocks (copper-containing blocks are safe for goats but toxic to sheep).

3. Common Diseases & Prevention

PPR is the greatest threat to small ruminants. It causes fever, mouth sores, nasal discharge, and severe diarrhea.

  • Vaccination: Follow your regional PPR vaccination schedule strictly.
  • Parasites: Worm infestations cause anemia and sudden weight loss. Monitor for pale mucous membranes and keep a strict deworming calendar.
Management Note: Never allow goats to graze in waterlogged or swampy areas, as these are hotbeds for liver fluke and other parasitic infestations.

4. Best Management Practices

Routine hoof trimming every 2-3 months is necessary to prevent foot rot, especially in humid climates.

Important: This guide offers general advice. Disease outbreaks can spread quickly; consult a local VFA if you notice group illness. Read our full Medical Disclaimer.
