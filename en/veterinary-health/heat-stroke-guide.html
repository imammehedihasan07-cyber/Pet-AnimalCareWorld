Heat Stroke in Dogs and Cats: Symptoms & First Aid

👨‍⚕️
Reviewed by: Imam Mehedi Hasan, VFA
Veterinary Field Assistant & Livestock Medical Practitioner
Last Updated: August 2026

Key Takeaways

  • Dogs and cats do not sweat like humans; they rely on panting to cool down.
  • Excessive panting, thick drool, and bright red gums are immediate warning signs.
  • Never use ice water to cool an overheated pet; it causes shock. Use cool (not cold) water instead.
  • Heat stroke is a severe medical emergency. First aid must be followed immediately by a trip to the vet.

During the hot summer months, heat stroke (hyperthermia) is one of the most common and fatal emergencies we see in veterinary practice. Because our pets cannot regulate their body temperature as efficiently as we can, a sunny day can turn deadly in a matter of minutes.

1. How Pets Regulate Body Temperature

Unlike humans, who have sweat glands all over their bodies, dogs and cats only have a few sweat glands located on their paw pads. To cool down, they rely almost entirely on panting. Panting evaporates moisture from their lungs, which takes heat away from their body. However, in high humidity or extreme heat, panting is not enough.

2. Warning Signs of Heat Stroke

A pet's normal body temperature is between 100°F and 102.5°F. If their temperature rises above 104°F, they are in danger. Watch for these signs:

  • Early Stages: Heavy, frantic panting; excessive drooling (saliva may look thick and ropey); bright red gums and tongue; increased heart rate.
  • Advanced Stages: Lethargy or weakness; vomiting or diarrhea (sometimes with blood); staggering (drunken gait); pale or blue gums; seizures or collapse.

3. Immediate First Aid Steps

If you suspect your pet is suffering from heat stroke, you must act immediately before driving to the vet. The goal is to lower their body temperature gradually.

  1. Move to a Cool Area: Immediately get the pet out of the heat and into an air-conditioned room or under a fan.
  2. Apply Cool Water: Wet their body with room-temperature or cool tap water. Focus on the back of the neck, armpits, and groin area.
  3. Offer Drinking Water: Offer cool water to drink, but do not force them to drink if they are unable to swallow or are vomiting.
Crucial First Aid Warning: NEVER use ice or ice-cold water to cool down a pet. Ice causes the blood vessels in the skin to constrict (shrink), which actually traps the heat inside the body's core and can trigger life-threatening shock.

4. How to Prevent Heat Stroke

Heat stroke is 100% preventable. Follow these guidelines during hot weather:

  • Never leave your pet in a parked car: Even with the windows cracked on a mild day, temperatures inside a car can exceed 120°F in less than 15 minutes.
  • Limit Exercise: Only walk your dog during the early morning or late evening when the pavement is cool. If the asphalt is too hot for your bare hand, it will burn their paws.
  • Provide Shade and Water: If your pet is outside, ensure they have access to deep shade and plenty of fresh, cool drinking water at all times.
