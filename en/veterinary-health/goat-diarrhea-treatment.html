Causes and Treatment of Diarrhea in Goats: Expert Guide

Diarrhea (scours) is one of the most frequent health issues in goat farming. While mild cases can be managed easily, severe diarrhea causes rapid dehydration and can lead to death within hours, especially in kids (baby goats). Field experience shows that immediate action saves lives.

1. Common Causes of Goat Diarrhea

Understanding the root cause helps in applying the correct treatment:

  • Parasitic Infections: Coccidiosis, worms, and stomach flukes are primary triggers for chronic scours.
  • Dietary Errors: Sudden changes in feed, overeating lush wet grass, or consuming spoiled feed.
  • Bacterial & Viral Infections: E. coli, Salmonella, or viral rotavirus outbreaks in young kids.

2. Preventing Fatal Dehydration

Dehydration is the primary killer in diarrheic goats. Electrolyte replacement is always the first priority.

  • Administer oral rehydration solutions (ORS) or custom electrolyte mixtures immediately.
  • Ensure clean, fresh water is available at all times.

3. Veterinary Treatments & Field Management

Depending on the diagnosis by a veterinary professional, treatment protocols include:

  • Anti-parasitic Drugs: Dewormers or specific anticoccidial medications if parasites are detected.
  • Antibiotics: Prescribed only if a secondary bacterial infection is present. Avoid indiscriminate use.
Field Emergency Warning: If a goat stops standing, has sunken eyes, and passes watery or bloody stool with a foul odor, seek emergency veterinary assistance immediately.

4. Prevention Strategies for Goat Farms

Keep your herd safe through proper sanitation:

  • Maintain clean, dry bedding and avoid overcrowded pens.
  • Follow a strict routine deworming and vaccination schedule under VFA supervision.
